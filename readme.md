# Enhanced Orthogonal Time-Frequency Space Modulation Based on Self-Encoder [with MATLAB+Python Code]

1. Abstract Orthogonal Time-Frequency Spacing (OTFS) is a novel waveform that demonstrates exceptional performance in dual-dispersion channels. Due to its ability to spread information symbols across the entire time-delay-Doppler plane, OTFS can achieve full diversity. However, the reliability of OTFS systems still needs to be improved to meet the stringent requirements of future communication systems. To address this issue, this paper proposes an enhanced OTFS (AEE-OTFS) modulation scheme based on an autoencoder (AE). By training the autoencoder under a Gaussian Noise Channel (AWGN), practical mapper and unmapper are obtained, thereby improving the bit error rate performance of OTFS systems and reducing detection complexity. The obtained mapper is used to map input bits into high-dimensional symbols, while the obtained unmapper recovers the information bits in the time-delay-Doppler domain. In addition, this paper also derives the theoretical upper bound for frame error rate (FER). Simulation results show that, under both ideal and non-ideal channel conditions, AEE-OTFS outperforms traditional OTFS in terms of FER. In addition to its outstanding bit error rate performance, AEE-OTFS also has lower decoding complexity.

Introduction High mobility communication is one of the expected characteristics in the IMT-2020 standard for 5G and future communication systems, as defined by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU), which includes enhancement mobile broadband (eMBB) and ultra-reliable low-latency communications (URLLC) applications [1]. Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM) has been widely used in 4G, 5G, and many other wireless communication standards due to its excellent performance in time-invariant frequency selective channels. However, in high mobility scenarios, wireless channels exhibit time-varying characteristics. The Doppler spread in such channels can cause inter-carrier interference (ICI), which degrades the performance of OFDM modulation. In recent years, Orthogonal Time-Frequency Space (OTFS) modulation has been proposed as a clever solution to this issue [2]. In OTFS modulation, information symbols are multiplexed in the delay-Doppler domain and distributed across the time-frequency domain. This process converts the dual-dispersion channel into an effectively non-fading virtual channel, allowing all information symbols to experience almost the same channel gain, thus enabling full diversity [2,3].
In recent years, deep learning (DL) methods have been applied in various fields including speech/image recognition, medical diagnosis, natural language processing, etc. With the aid of deep learning, powerful computing capabilities enable computers to automatically learn data characteristics. Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) possess excellent feature extraction capabilities through their multi-layer structure. Typically, DNNs process data in complex ways through advanced mathematical modeling. Moreover, DNNs have been successfully applied to wireless communication systems [4]. For example, techniques based on deep neural networks can be implemented in various modules of the transceiver process, such as encoding, channel estimation, equalization, modulation, and detection. In OTFS modulation, literature [5] and [6] have proposed signal detection techniques based on deep neural networks. Additionally, in [7], the authors propose a clever precoding technique based on deep neural networks that ensures good reliability without instantaneous channel state information (CSI).
The Encoder-Decoder (AE) architecture is used to treat the transmitter and receiver of a communication system as their respective encoders and decoders [8]. Therefore, by training this AE-based structure, it is possible to learn the transmitter and receiver. The encoder learns a representation for a given data sample set, while the decoder aims to reconstruct the original data samples from this representation. In OFDM modulation, a study in the literature trained an AE-based scheme to learn the transmitter and receiver structures of an OFDM system [9]. On the other hand, in OTFS modulation, authors proposed a deep neural network-based AE architecture, where the encoder was trained to reduce the Peak-to-Average Power Ratio (PAPR), while the decoder was trained to reconstruct the original signal [10]. However, in [10], the entire OTFS frame was input into the AE, leading to a significant increase in computational complexity with frame size.
Research has shown that for larger frame sizes, OTFS systems can provide equal diversity gain as the number of paths in a dual-dispersion channel [3]. Although traditional OTFS performs well in high mobility environments, more complex OTFS-based modulation schemes are still needed to meet the stringent requirements of 6G standards [11,12]. For example, to improve the bit error rate performance of OTFS, a scheme maximizing Squared Minimum Euclidean Distance (SMED) could be designed.
This paper proposes an enhanced OTFS (AEE-OTFS) scheme based on self-encoding networks, where the mapper and learned demapper of the OTFS system are modeled as the encoder and decoder of a self-encoding network respectively. In this scheme, the self-encoding network is trained offline in an AWGN channel, allowing the encoder to generate a set of n-dimensional symbols that maximize the Euclidean distance between symbol pairs. Additionally, the decoder at the receiving end can perform low-complexity detection of the n-dimensional symbols of the balanced OTFS signal. It is important to note that while learning modulator and demodulator modules using the OTFS framework under dual fading channels is possible, it does not necessitate training the self-encoding network in an AWGN channel, which could lead to a more complex structure and increased training time. Unlike [10], the proposed self-encoding-based scheme trains in an AWGN channel without including signal processing modules for dual fading channels and OTFS transceivers. It should be noted that the learned mapper (LM) and demapper (LD) modules obtained from training in an AWGN channel will be applied to actual OTFS systems that experience dual fading channels. Moreover, the core goal of this paper is to improve bit error rate performance, whereas [10] focuses on reducing PAPR. Compared to [10], the proposed scheme does not utilize the entire OTFS frame to train the self-encoding network, thus resulting in a lower complexity of the self-encoding network structure.

Conclusion: This paper proposes an autoencoder-based scheme to improve the bit error rate performance of OTFS by learning a set of n-dimensional symbols and maximizing the squared minimum Euclidean distance between symbols. During training, an AWGN channel was used instead of a dual dispersion channel to construct a simple autoencoder. After training the encoder and decoder in the AWGN channel, they were used as mapping and unmapping modules in the actual OTFS system. In addition, this paper derives the theoretical upper bound for frame error rate. Computer simulation results show that the AEE-OTFS outperforms traditional OTFS systems in terms of frame error rate performance. Moreover, the learned unmapping module (LD) significantly reduces the decoding complexity. Overall, the proposed AEE-OTFS scheme, with its excellent bit error rate performance and good flexibility in high mobility scenarios, is expected to be a candidate solution for certain applications in 6G. Future research will focus on optimizing the proposed system in terms of bit error rate.
